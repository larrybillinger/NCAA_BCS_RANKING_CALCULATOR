METHOD NOTES

Transparent by design

The public site explains both the ranking formula and the prediction layer rather than hiding a black-box score.

Ranking formula

For a Division I opponent ranked R in a pool of N teams, the actual scoring margin is always points for − points against. There is no longer a separate +10 for winning.

Home or neutral win = (N + 1 - R) + scoring margin
Away win            = (N + 1 - R) + scoring margin + 7

Away or neutral loss = (-R) + scoring margin
Home loss            = (-R) + scoring margin - 7

The seven-point site adjustment rewards winning on the road and penalizes losing at home. A home win gets no extra site points, and an away loss gets no extra site penalty.

Season ranking value

Each completed game keeps its frozen game score. The season ranking value is the arithmetic mean of those frozen game scores:

Ranking score = sum of frozen game scores / games played

This prevents an extra game from automatically creating more ranking points and makes a bye week neutral. Raw cumulative points are retained only for auditing. Exact average-score ties use head-to-head, then win percentage, then average opponent strength before the deterministic name fallback.

Every Division I team enters its first game on the same neutral T-1 baseline because it has no current-season evidence yet. For scoring, that baseline is the average occupied rank (N + 1) / 2; in the current {{ team_count }}-team pool that is {{ neutral_rank|num }}. Provider Week 0 games are treated as ranking Week 1. A team that has not played yet stays on the neutral baseline for opponent scoring; after its first completed game, the immediately preceding completed week's scoring rank takes over. Exact score ties share the average rank of their occupied positions. Earlier games stay frozen.

FBS, FCS, and conference views

There is one national ranking. The FBS, FCS, and conference views do not re-rate anyone; they take the national Division I order, remove the teams outside the selected group, and number the rest 1, 2, 3 without gaps. A conference view is ordered by national rank, not by conference record.

FCS modifier

FBS and FCS are both Division I. The percentages below describe how much of the normal game score each side receives.

FBS vs FBS      = 100%
FBS vs FCS      = 100%
FCS vs FCS      = 50%
FCS loss to FBS = 50%
FCS win over FBS = 100%

The FCS percentage applies to opponent-rank points and scoring margin. The seven-point site adjustment is applied afterward at full value, so every road win gets +7 and every home loss gets -7.

Score prediction

The official predictor is hybrid_core_v1. It combines two current-season signals: 40% of the calibrated numerical rank-gap margin and 60% of an offense/defense matchup margin. The offense and defense profiles use only completed current-season games and are shrunk toward the Division I scoring mean by three pseudo-games so one early result cannot dominate the projection.

The matchup total comes from the two teams' shrunk scoring profiles. The blended margin is then split around that total to produce projected team scores and a win probability. Because offense/defense information is now part of the official prediction, the model may project a lower-ranked team to win when its current-season matchup profile outweighs the ranking-gap signal. This affects predictions only; it never changes the national ranking formula.

Displayed integer scores use winner-consistent half-up rounding. If the two rounded scores would tie while the modeled margin is non-zero, the modeled winner receives one additional display point. A true zero-margin projection may still display a tie. This display rule does not change the continuous projected margin, total, or win probability.

Home-field, travel, altitude, weather, stadium-demand, and other context adjustments are not part of hybrid_core_v1. Those remain separate research ideas until they are validated from pre-kickoff data.

Locked predictions

The newest hybrid projection available before kickoff becomes the official prediction for that game and is never replaced after the result is known. Official predictions created under the retired rank_gap_v3 predictor remain stored exactly as they were, but the active Game Book and Accuracy ledger use hybrid_core_v1 after promotion.

For games played before the hybrid model was official, the Game Book can show a clearly labeled research retrocast built only from the ranking snapshot and completed-game scoring information available before that game. Retrocasts never count toward the official locked accuracy ledger.

Data source

Schedules, scores, classifications, and game-team statistics are retrieved server-side from CollegeFootballData.com and privately normalized into PostgreSQL. The public site exposes derived rankings, predictions, and ordinary factual displays rather than a bulk copy of the provider feed.
