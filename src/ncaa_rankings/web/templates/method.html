METHOD NOTES

Transparent by design

The public site explains both the ranking formula and the prediction layer rather than hiding a black-box score.

Ranking formula

For a Division I opponent ranked R in a pool of N teams:

Win  = (N + 1 - R) + 10 + scoring margin
Loss = (-R) + scoring margin

Before Week 1, every Division I team is tied because no current-season evidence exists. For scoring, that all-team tie uses the average occupied rank (N + 1) / 2; with 266 teams that is 133.5. Beginning in Week 2, each game uses the immediately preceding completed week's scoring rank. Exact score ties share the average rank of their occupied positions. Earlier games stay frozen.

FCS modifier

FBS team vs Division I opponent = 100%
FCS vs FCS                      = 50%
FCS loss to FBS                 = 50%
FCS win over FBS                = 100%

Score prediction

The prediction layer is monotonic: the higher-ranked team is always the projected winner. The projected margin is the ranking gap multiplied by a current-season points-per-rank scale. That scale is calibrated from completed games, blended conservatively early in the season, and constrained so it cannot reverse the ranking order. Home field and free intercept adjustments do not change the projected winner. The season's average scoring total is used only to turn the projected margin into a projected final score.

Locked predictions

The newest projection available before kickoff becomes the official prediction for that game. It is never replaced after the result is known. A later ranking may produce a retrocast, but that is a separate research view and never changes official accuracy.

Data source

Schedules, scores, classifications, and game-team statistics are retrieved server-side from CollegeFootballData.com and privately normalized into PostgreSQL. The public site exposes derived rankings, predictions, and ordinary factual displays rather than a bulk copy of the provider feed.
